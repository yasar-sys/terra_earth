# TerraBangla — Final 240-Second Voiceover Script (English)

**For:** NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 Bangladesh — “Be An Earth System Trend Detective!”  
**Team:** MEC TERRA_DETECTORS  
**Project:** TerraBangla  
**Target runtime:** 4:00 (240 seconds)  
**Delivery:** Warm, confident, curious; approximately 140 words per minute.  
**Direction:** Read only the narration. Allow the marked pauses and let each screen transition finish before continuing.

---

## 00:00–00:22 — Opening and identity

**On screen:** TerraBangla logo, then the Earth appears.

**Narration:**  
Every place has a climate story. But a single hot day, one heavy storm, or one unusual season cannot tell us how that story is changing. We need evidence collected over many years. [PAUSE] This is TerraBangla, built by MEC TERRA_DETECTORS for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026. It helps people investigate Bangladesh through real Earth-observation records.
Instead of asking learners to accept a conclusion, it invites them to inspect the pattern, question the result, and follow the source.

---

## 00:22–00:48 — From the globe to Bangladesh

**On screen:** Rotate the globe, reveal the highlighted Bangladesh area, then select it.

**Narration:**  
Our journey begins in space. The globe lets us see Bangladesh as part of one connected Earth system. Select the country, and the view moves smoothly from the planet to a detailed map. From here, every one of Bangladesh’s sixty-four districts can become the starting point of an investigation.

---

## 00:48–01:27 — District evidence

**On screen:** Select a district and move through vegetation, land temperature, air temperature, sunlight, and rainfall.

**Narration:**  
Choose a district, then choose what you want to study: vegetation, land-surface temperature, air temperature, sunlight, or rainfall. TerraBangla reads pre-cached NASA records and shows the earlier value, the recent value, the unit, and the long-term rate of change. A Mann–Kendall test checks whether the pattern is statistically meaningful. A Theil–Sen estimate measures the rate without letting one unusual year control the result. If the evidence is not significant, TerraBangla says so. If a district record is unavailable, it says, “Data not yet available.” It never fills a gap with an invented number.

---

## 01:27–01:55 — National heatmap

**On screen:** Open the heatmap, switch variables, and move through years.

**Narration:**  
The heatmap widens the investigation. Instead of one district, we can examine gridded NASA observations across Bangladesh. Move through the years or switch between temperature, rainfall, and sunlight. The colors change because the underlying measurements change—not because an illustration is guessing what happened.

---

## 01:55–02:30 — Comparison and downloadable evidence

**On screen:** Compare two districts, then show trend lines, confidence bands, verdict, and PDF export.

**Narration:**  
Next, the comparison workspace turns observation into a testable question. Compare the same variable in two districts, or compare two variables within one district. Choose the start and end years. Each line includes its trend estimate, confidence band, p-value, and significance result. The verdict describes only what the analysis supports. Students and researchers can then export the comparison as a PDF, keeping the evidence, methods, and sources together.

---

## 02:30–02:57 — Grounded AI assistance

**On screen:** Open Ask TerraBangla or the Evidence Lab and enter a student observation.

**Narration:**  
Students can also describe a pattern in their own words. TerraBangla’s AI assistant responds with a possible scientific explanation and suggests a useful follow-up comparison. But the assistant never receives authority to create climate facts. Its numerical evidence is recomputed on the server from the cached records, and possible explanations remain clearly separate from measured results.

---

## 02:57–03:29 — Learning studio for children

**On screen:** Enter “Bangladesh in My Hands,” choose districts, answer a trend question, then reveal the answer and learning takeaway.

**Narration:**  
For younger learners, “Bangladesh in My Hands” turns the same evidence into a calm, bilingual learning studio. Each district has its own geography-inspired visual atmosphere, while the numbers still come only from NASA records. Children compare earlier and recent measurements, answer a short observation question, see the correct answer, and read what the evidence teaches. A gentle detective guide supports the activity, but never pretends to be data. English and Bangla remain available throughout.

---

## 03:29–03:50 — Trust, access, and openness

**On screen:** Open a provenance panel, show keyboard navigation, phone layout, install icon, and data sources.

**Narration:**  
Every chart includes provenance: the dataset, source link, retrieval time, and whether the record came from cache. TerraBangla is keyboard accessible, supports reduced motion, works on small screens, and can be installed on a phone. Its code is open under the Apache Two Point Zero license, and its datasets are documented for anyone to review.

---

## 03:50–04:00 — Closing

**On screen:** Return to the TerraBangla logo and team name.

**Narration:**  
Climate understanding begins with better questions and honest evidence. TerraBangla helps Bangladesh’s next generation explore both. [PAUSE] TerraBangla, by MEC TERRA_DETECTORS. Be an Earth System Trend Detective.
From the whole planet to one local district, every investigation begins with curiosity and ends with evidence that can be checked.
